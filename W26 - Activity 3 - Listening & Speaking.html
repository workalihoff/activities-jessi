Phase 1 — Key Phrases

Seven phrases you'll hear in the episode. They're all about one thing: making sure everybody understands. All examples are from the real episode.

get your wires crossed
When two people understand the same thing in different ways — a misunderstanding.
Pippa"We've got our wires crossed there and we've misunderstood each other."
Can I just check that I've understood that right?
A polite way to confirm you understood a task or instruction.
Phil"You could just ask them to explain it again, you know, 'Can I just check that I've understood that right?'"
Could I just clarify…?
To clarify = to make something clearer. Use it to ask about one specific detail.
Pippa"You could also say 'I'm sorry, but could I just clarify?'"
walk me through / talk me through
Explain something to me step by step.
Pippa"'Can you walk me through how you usually do this?'"
Phil"Can you just talk me through that again?"
I'm not sure I follow you.
A polite way to say "I don't understand what you're saying."
Phil"'I'm not sure I follow you. Can you just talk me through that again?'"
Do you mean…?
Repeat what you think the person said, in your own words, to check.
Pippa"I just want to be sure I've got you 100% right. Do you mean…"
Does that make sense? · just let me know
Check that other people understood you.
Phil"We can always ask other people 'Does that make sense?'"
Pippa"If any of this doesn't make sense, let me know."
💡 How to use these phrases to get information
Some of these phrases are polite starters. When you need information, don't stop there — add a question word (who, what, when, which, how many…).
✗ Could I just clarify the deadline? → "Sure. It's Friday." (Friday when? What time?)
✓ Could I just clarify — what time is the deadline on Friday?

Phase 4 — Ask the Right Question

First, a quick toolkit for questions. Then use it in a voice note to Marcus.

🧰 Question Toolkit

Choose the question word for the information you need.

WordAsks about…Example
Whoa personWho is joining the call?
Whata thing / informationWhat time is the meeting?
Whichone option from a groupWhich file is the final version?
Whena time or dateWhen is the deadline?
Whya reasonWhy did the payment fail?
Howthe way / the processHow do you usually do this?
How manythings you can count: files, people, days, transactionsHow many transactions failed?
How muchthings you can't count: time, money, work, informationHow much time do we have?
How longdurationHow long does the review take?

📐 Word order

Question word+is / are / do / does / did / can / will+person / thing+verb…?
✓ When is the meeting?
✓ How many files do we need?
✓ What did the client say?
✗ When the meeting is?
✗ How many files we need?
✗ What the client said?

🎯 Yes/no questions vs. question-word questions

A yes/no question only gets you yes or no. A question word gets you the information you need.

Is the meeting on Tuesday?
→ "No." 🤷 (You still don't know when it is!)
When is the meeting?
→ "Thursday at 3." ✓
Are there many files?
→ "Yes." 🤷
How many files are there?
→ "About 50." ✓
💡 Bonus: When who or what is the person/thing that did the action, don't use do/did: "Who sent the file?" ✓ — not "Who did send the file?"
🤝 Make it polite with a phrase from Phase 1: "Sorry, could I just clarify — which file is the final version?" · "Can I just check — when is the deadline?"
