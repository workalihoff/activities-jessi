Phase 1 — Learn the Patterns

Two small words that carry a lot of weight. Read carefully — these show up constantly in real conversation.

preposition + PRONOUN

Object Pronouns After Prepositions

Definition
A pronoun that comes right after a preposition (for, to, with, about, from...) must be in the object form — never the subject form.
Examples in context
The report is ready for him. (not "for he")
📍 Marcus is waiting on it.
I sent the file to them. (not "to they")
📍 Vinicio and Priya both needed it.
Can you follow up with her? (not "with she")
📍 Asking about a colleague.

⚠️ Quick test

If there's a preposition right before the pronoun, it's always object form. "For he" and "for they" never sound right in English — no exceptions.

preposition + TIME

"Only on Fridays" — Preposition + Day

Definition
When talking about a specific day of the week (especially as a repeated routine), use "on" + the plural day — not "the" + singular.
Examples in context
We only have meetings on Fridays. (not "only the Friday")
I work from home on Mondays. (not "on the Monday" for routines)
⚠️ Note the exception

"The Friday" is only correct when pointing to one specific, already-known Friday — "I'll see you the Friday before the deadline." For routines and general schedules, always "on Fridays."
