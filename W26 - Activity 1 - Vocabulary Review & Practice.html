Phase 1 — Your Phrase Bank

No new words this week! These are the 12 phrases you already know. Read them again — especially the comparison table. Then you'll use them all together.

🤝 Group A — Handoffs & people
touch base (with someone)
A short, informal check-in to see how things are going.
"I'll touch base with the vendor tomorrow."
sync up (with someone on something)
Meet or talk to agree on details. Both people share information.
"Let's sync up on the test results before Friday."
loop someone in (on something)
Add someone to an email thread or conversation from now on.
"Can you loop Legal in on this thread?"
bring someone up to speed (on something)
Give someone the information they missed.
"I'll bring the new analyst up to speed on the project."
hand off (something to someone)
Pass your work or responsibility to another person.
"I'm handing off the reports to Ana next week."
point of contact
The person people should talk to about something.
"Luis is the point of contact for any vendor questions."

🔍 The four people phrases — side by side

These four are easy to mix up. Ask yourself the question in the last column.

PhraseWhat happensHow long?Ask yourself…
touch baseQuick check-in. "How's it going?"Minutes, or one messageIs it just a quick check?
sync upAgree on details. Both people share.A real conversation or meetingDo we both need to agree on details?
loop inAdd someone to the conversation.From now onIs someone missing from the conversation?

💡 Touch base vs. sync up: "I'll touch base with Ana" = a short message to check how she's doing. "I'll sync up with Ana" = we'll talk and agree on the plan together.

📍 Watch the word order

With bring … up to speed and loop … in, the person goes in the middle.

✓ I'll bring Ana up to speed.
✓ I'll bring her up to speed.
✗ I'll bring up to speed to Ana.
✗ I'll bring up to speed her.
✓ Let's loop Treasury in on the project.
✓ Let's loop them in.
✗ Let's loop in Treasury with the project.
💡 Keep it short: a sync-up already means a short meeting. Say "Let's schedule a sync-up" — not "a sync-up meeting."
⏰ Group B — Deadlines & planning
feasible
Realistic, possible to do.
"Is Tuesday feasible for your team?"
firm deadline
A deadline that cannot move.
"The audit date is a firm deadline."
ballpark figure
A rough estimate — not exact.
"Just a ballpark figure: about two weeks."
contingency
Extra time in a plan, in case something goes wrong.
"I added two days of contingency."
pencil in
Reserve a date, but it's not confirmed yet.
"Let's pencil in Monday at 3."
close the loop
Confirm clearly so nobody is unsure.
"Just closing the loop: the file is approved."
